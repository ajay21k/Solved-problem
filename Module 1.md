# Kinematics (Motion)

**Interview Level: Beginner → Advanced (Juspay, Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Flipkart)**

---

# 1. What is Kinematics?

Kinematics is the branch of physics that **describes motion without considering the forces causing it**.

It answers questions like:

- How far did an object travel?
- How fast is it moving?
- How long did it take?
- Is it speeding up or slowing down?

It does **not** explain _why_ the object moves—that belongs to dynamics (Newton's Laws).

---

# 2. Real-Life Examples

- A bike traveling from home to college.
- A train moving between stations.
- A cricket ball thrown into the air.
- A car accelerating from a traffic signal.
- An elevator moving upward.

---

# 3. Basic Terms

## (a) Rest

An object is at rest if its position does not change with time.

Example:  
A parked car.

---

## (b) Motion

An object is in motion if its position changes with time.

Example:  
A moving bus.

# 5. Distance vs Displacement

|Distance|Displacement|
|---|---|
|Total path travelled|Shortest distance from start to end|
|Scalar|Vector|
|Never negative|Can be positive, negative, or zero|

### Example

A person walks:

- 5 m east
- 5 m west

Distance = 10 m

Displacement = 0 m

# 6. Speed

Speed tells **how fast an object moves**.

Formula:

Speed=DistanceTime\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}Speed=TimeDistance​

Units:

- m/s (SI)
- km/h

### Conversion

- 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h
- 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s

**Shortcut:** Multiply by **18/5** to convert m/s → km/h, and by **5/18** for km/h → m/s.

---

# 7. Velocity

Velocity is speed **with direction**.

Formula:

Velocity=DisplacementTime\text{Velocity}=\frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}}Velocity=TimeDisplacement​

Example:  
A car moves 20 m east in 4 s.

Velocity = 20/4 = **5 m/s east**

---

Average Speed=Total DistanceTotal Time\text{Average Speed}=\frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}}Average Speed=Total TimeTotal Distance​

Average Speed=2uvu+v\boxed{\text{Average Speed}=\frac{2uv}{u+v}}Average Speed=u+v2uv​​

# 9. Average Velocity

Formula:

Average Velocity=Total DisplacementTotal Time\text{Average Velocity}=\frac{\text{Total Displacement}}{\text{Total Time}}Average Velocity=Total TimeTotal Displacement​

If displacement is zero, average velocity is zero even though average speed is not.

# 10. Acceleration

Acceleration is the **rate of change of velocity**.

a=v−uta=\frac{v-u}{t}a=tv−u​

Where:

- uuu = Initial velocity
- vvv = Final velocity
- ttt = Time

Unit:

- m/s²

### Types

- Positive acceleration
- Negative acceleration (deceleration)
- Zero acceleration
- Uniform acceleration

---

# 11. Uniform and Non-Uniform Motion

### Uniform Motion

Speed remains constant.

Example:  
Train moving steadily at 60 km/h.

### Non-Uniform Motion

Speed changes with time.

Example:  
A car in city traffic.

# 14. Motion Graphs

## Distance-Time Graph

- Straight line → Constant speed
- Steeper line → Higher speed
- Horizontal line → At rest

**Slope = Speed**

---

## Velocity-Time Graph

- Horizontal line → Constant velocity
- Increasing line → Acceleration
- Decreasing line → Deceleration

**Slope = Acceleration**

**Area under graph = Displacement**

---

## Acceleration-Time Graph

- Horizontal line → Constant acceleration

**Area under graph = Change in velocity**

# 16. Vertical Motion

Thrown upward:

- Velocity decreases while rising.
- At the highest point, velocity = 0.
- Gravity acts downward throughout.

Useful formulas:

Time to reach maximum height:

t=ugt=\frac{u}{g}t=gu​

Maximum height:

H=u22gH=\frac{u^2}{2g}H=2gu2​

Total time of flight:

T=2ugT=\frac{2u}{g}T=g2u​

# 18. Common Mistakes

- Confusing distance with displacement.
- Confusing speed with velocity.
- Forgetting direction in vector quantities.
- Mixing km/h and m/s without conversion.
- Using equations of motion when acceleration is **not constant**.
- Using distance instead of displacement in velocity calculations.

- **Equations of Motion:** v=u+atv=u+atv=u+at, s=ut+12at2s=ut+\frac12at^2s=ut+21​at2, v2=u2+2asv^2=u^2+2asv2=u2+2as
